About

I have over four years of research experience in a university business‑school environment, complemented by experience in African Studies, stakeholder engagement, field research, qualitative data collection, research reporting, and project coordination.

I am currently developing a doctoral research agenda examining how institutions, development policies, and economic structures shape the capacity of African economic actors to participate in processes of structural transformation and inclusive development, with a particular interest in Ghana and West Africa public policy, economic participation, and institutions.

Research

Current Research Agenda

Institutions, Economic Participation, and Structural Transformation in Africa

A central question emerging from my research is: How do institutions, development policies, and economic structures shape the capacity of African economic actors to participate in structural transformation and inclusive development? I am particularly interested in the relationship between policy and economic agency: how entrepreneurs, firms, producers, and other locally rooted economic actors experience and navigate institutional environments, and under what conditions their participation can contribute to broader processes of economic transformation.

This agenda brings together several areas of interest: African development and structural transformation Institutions and development policy Entrepreneurship and economic participation Informal and locally rooted economies Economic ecosystems and networks African political economy Policy implementation and institutional change Ghana and West Africa African diaspora and transnational economic connections

Rather than treating entrepreneurship as an isolated individual activity, I am interested in understanding the institutional and structural conditions in which economic actors operate. This includes questions of access to markets, finance, networks, policy support, productive capabilities, and opportunities for upgrading and participation in higher-value economic activity. The precise empirical and sectoral focus of this research is currently being developed through engagement with the literature and potential doctoral supervisors.

Research Background

My doctoral research interests build on a longer trajectory of research across Africa and its diaspora.

African Languages and African Studies

My academic training began with the study of African languages, with a particular focus on Gã, a Ghanaian language. I completed a First Class Honours BA in Linguistics and Arabic at the University of Ghana and an MA in Linguistics at Western University. My linguistic research involved field-based work with Gã speakers and West African communities, as well as research into grammatical structure, language use, and documentation. This work resulted in peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations, including research presented at the Canadian Linguistic Association Congress, the Linguistic Society of America Linguistics Institute, and the Conference on African Linguistics. This training developed my experience in field research, qualitative data collection, linguistic analysis, research design, and scholarly writing, while also grounding my academic work in African contexts.

Black Entrepreneurship and Economic Participation

My research subsequently expanded toward questions of economic participation, entrepreneurship, and institutional ecosystems. At the Sprott School of Business at Carleton University, I spent several years conducting research on the Black entrepreneurship ecosystem in Canada. This work included literature review, systematic evidence synthesis, ecosystem mapping, research analysis, scholarly writing, and research dissemination. A major outcome of this research was a systematic review examining the Black entrepreneurship ecosystem and the role of networks in advancing Black entrepreneurship in Canada, published by Springer in 2026. This research has deepened my interest in how economic actors interact with institutions, networks, policies, and broader economic structures, and how these relationships shape opportunities for economic participation and development.

Community-Based Research and Knowledge Mobilization

My current work with the Black Entrepreneurship Knowledge Hub further connects research with communities, policy, and practice. My role involves contributing to a national, community-focused research platform addressing evidence gaps in Black entrepreneurship. This work includes research and knowledge mobilization activities aimed at translating evidence into accessible and practically relevant insights concerning business growth, resilience, financing, investment readiness, digital adoption, and interventions. This experience has strengthened my interest in the relationship between research evidence, community knowledge, policy, and implementation—and in how research can contribute to understanding development challenges while remaining responsive to the experiences of the communities being studied.

Research Approach

My research training combines qualitative, field-based, literature-based, and applied research approaches. Methods and approaches I have worked with include: Systematic and structured literature reviews Qualitative research Field research and primary data collection Interviews and focus groups Surveys and participant recruitment Qualitative data analysis Research synthesis and interpretation Ecosystem and stakeholder mapping Policy- and stakeholder-oriented research Research reporting and scholarly writing Knowledge mobilization and translation of research findings I have experience with research and analytical tools including NVivo, Qualtrics, R, ArcGIS/QGIS, Praat, Zotero, and other research-management and data-analysis tools. My methodological interests are intentionally interdisciplinary. I am particularly interested in combining policy and institutional analysis with qualitative research and evidence from economic actors themselves, while using relevant secondary data and sectoral evidence where appropriate.

Geographic Focus

My primary geographic interest is Ghana and West Africa, informed by my academic background, research experience, and longstanding engagement with African studies. At the same time, my work on Black entrepreneurship in Canada has given me a complementary perspective on African diaspora communities, transnational economic relationships, and the movement of knowledge, networks, and resources across contexts. I am interested in how these different scales—local, national, regional, and transnational—can be brought together in research on African development.

Research in Progress

I am currently developing a doctoral research project focused on African development, institutions, economic participation, and structural transformation, with Ghana and West Africa as an initial geographic focus. The project is being developed through engagement with scholarship in African Studies, Development Studies, political economy, entrepreneurship, institutional analysis, and economic development. I am particularly interested in research that connects macro-level institutions and development policy with the experiences and strategies of economic actors operating within those systems. The specific research questions, empirical cases, and methodological design will continue to develop through doctoral supervision and engagement with the relevant literature.
