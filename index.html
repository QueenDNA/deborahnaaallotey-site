About

I am an interdisciplinary researcher with academic and applied research experience spanning African languages and linguistics, African studies, Black entrepreneurship, economic participation, and community‑based research.

I hold an MA in Linguistics and a First Class Honours BA in Linguistics and Arabic, with peer‑reviewed publications on the syntax of Gã, a Ghanaian language, as well as research on Black entrepreneurship in Canada.

I have over four years of research experience in a university business‑school environment, complemented by experience in African Studies, stakeholder engagement, field research, qualitative data collection, research reporting, and project coordination.

I am currently developing a doctoral research agenda examining how institutions, development policies, and economic structures shape the capacity of African economic actors to participate in processes of structural transformation and inclusive development, with a particular interest in Ghana and West Africa and in the roles of public policy, institutions, and economic participation in development.

Research

My doctoral research interests focus on Ghana and West Africa, examining how public policy and institutions shape economic participation and opportunities for inclusive development and structural transformation.

Current Research Agenda

Institutions, Public Policy, and Economic Participation in West Africa

I am interested in how institutions and development policies shape the ability of African economic actors—including entrepreneurs, firms, producers, and other locally rooted actors—to participate in processes of economic transformation. My current thinking focuses particularly on the relationship between policy and economic agency: how economic actors navigate institutional environments, and under what conditions their participation can contribute to broader development outcomes.

Key areas of interest include:

  • Public policy and institutions
  • Economic participation and entrepreneurship
  • Structural transformation and inclusive development
  • Informal and locally rooted economies
  • Economic networks and ecosystems
  • Ghana and West Africa

    • Research Background

    My doctoral research interests build on a longer trajectory of research across Africa and its diaspora.

    African Languages and African Studies

    My academic training began with the study of African languages, with a particular focus on Gã, a Ghanaian language. I completed a First Class Honours BA in Linguistics and Arabic at the University of Ghana and an MA in Linguistics at Western University. My linguistic research involved field-based work with Gã speakers and West African communities, as well as research into grammatical structure, language use, and documentation. This work resulted in peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations, including research presented at the Canadian Linguistic Association Congress, the Linguistic Society of America Linguistics Institute, and the Conference on African Linguistics. This training developed my experience in field research, qualitative data collection, linguistic analysis, research design, and scholarly writing, while also grounding my academic work in African contexts.

    Black Entrepreneurship and Economic Participation

    My research subsequently expanded toward questions of economic participation, entrepreneurship, and institutional ecosystems. At the Sprott School of Business at Carleton University, I spent several years conducting research on the Black entrepreneurship ecosystem in Canada. This work included literature review, systematic evidence synthesis, ecosystem mapping, research analysis, scholarly writing, and research dissemination. A major outcome of this research was a systematic review examining the Black entrepreneurship ecosystem and the role of networks in advancing Black entrepreneurship in Canada, published by Springer in 2026. This research has deepened my interest in how economic actors interact with institutions, networks, policies, and broader economic structures, and how these relationships shape opportunities for economic participation and development.

    Community-Based Research and Knowledge Mobilization

    My current work with the Black Entrepreneurship Knowledge Hub further connects research with communities, policy, and practice. My role involves contributing to a national, community-focused research platform addressing evidence gaps in Black entrepreneurship. This work includes research and knowledge mobilization activities aimed at translating evidence into accessible and practically relevant insights concerning business growth, resilience, financing, investment readiness, digital adoption, and interventions. This experience has strengthened my interest in the relationship between research evidence, community knowledge, policy, and implementation—and in how research can contribute to understanding development challenges while remaining responsive to the experiences of the communities being studied.

    Research Approach

    My research training combines qualitative, field-based, literature-based, and applied research approaches. Methods and approaches I have worked with include:

  • Systematic and structured literature reviews
  • Qualitative research
  • Field research and primary data collection
  • Interviews and focus groups
  • Surveys and participant recruitment
  • Qualitative data analysis
  • Research synthesis and interpretation
  • Ecosystem and stakeholder mapping
  • Policy- and stakeholder-oriented research
  • Research reporting and scholarly writing
  • Knowledge mobilization and translation of research findings

    • I have experience with research and analytical tools including NVivo, Qualtrics, R, ArcGIS/QGIS, Praat, Zotero, and other research-management and data-analysis tools.

    My methodological interests are intentionally interdisciplinary. I am particularly interested in combining policy and institutional analysis with qualitative research and evidence from economic actors themselves, while using relevant secondary data and sectoral evidence where appropriate.

    Geographic Focus

    My primary geographic focus is Ghana and West Africa, informed by my academic background, research experience, and longstanding engagement with African Studies. My research on Black entrepreneurship in Canada also provides a complementary perspective on African diaspora communities and transnational economic relationships.

    Research in Progress

    I am currently refining a doctoral research project on public policy, institutions, and economic participation in Ghana and West Africa. The empirical focus and methodological design will continue to develop through engagement with the literature and prospective doctoral supervision.
